# Jobs to Be Done — Cintelytics / Terminal

> Grounded in: icp.md (Jun 2026 CRM sprint, ~50 orgs, ~130 email events)
> Every claim tagged with source. `(assumption)` = unverified.

---

## Core job statement

> **When** a high-stakes political event or media moment is approaching,
> **I want to** know which narratives and frames are gaining or losing traction —
> before they surface in mainstream coverage —
> **so I can** respond, publish, advise, or brief my campaign faster than
> my competitor, opponent, or peer.

This is the single job every piece of outreach copy must serve.
If a subject line, opening sentence, or CTA does not connect to this job,
rewrite it until it does.

---

## Job layers

### Functional job (what they're literally trying to do)
Track the velocity and direction of political and media narratives in real time,
across sources, with enough lead time to act on the signal.

Current tools that are failing at this job: manual monitoring, search alerts,
RSS feeds, journalist networks. These are too slow, too shallow, or too
relationship-dependent. [CRM: hooks in tracker — "story gaps," "quote mismatches +
framing divergence," "issue ownership"]

### Emotional job (how they want to feel)
Confident and ahead of the curve. Not reactive. The one who caught the story
first and had the brief ready before anyone asked. [CRM: synthesised from
reply tone — Brit Stakston (Blankspot) rescheduled proactively; Rolf van den Brink
(Dagensopinion) replied late evening same day — these are people who want to
be first, not fast followers]

### Social job (how they want to be seen)
The analyst, editor, or comms lead who doesn't miss things. In Swedish media
and PA circles, being early to a narrative is a professional identity signal —
it's how you justify your position and your budget. [CRM: assumption, grounded
in the seniority of contacts who engaged: editorial directors, MDs, press secretaries]

## Trigger (when the job becomes urgent)

A specific high-stakes event is approaching and their current tools
feel dangerously inadequate.

**Documented triggers from CRM:**
- Almedalen week (late June / early July) — single biggest activation
- Any election cycle, budget week, or major policy announcement [assumption]
- A competitor or opponent published something they missed [assumption — not in CRM,
  but consistent with the emotional job]

**What this means for copy:**
Name the event. Name the deadline. Urgency tied to a date converts 3–5x faster
than evergreen framing. [CRM: Blankspot demo 3 days, MPF 2 days, Resumé 4 days —
all Almedalen-framed; contrast with generic outreach that got no demo bookings]

## Outcome statements (what "done" looks like for them)

Write copy that names at least one of these outcomes — not the product:

1. "Know which frame is winning before it's the consensus" [CRM: tracker hook]
2. "Catch narrative shifts before they reach mainstream coverage"
3. "Respond to your client's question before they finish asking it"
4. "Brief your team in minutes, not a morning of monitoring"
5. "Own the story gap your competitor hasn't spotted yet" [CRM: tracker hook]

---

## Obstacle (what blocks them today)

The copy must name the obstacle before offering the unlock.
The obstacle is not "bad tools" — it's the *consequence* of bad tools:

- Reading the news the same morning everyone else does → no lead time
- Manual search returns volume, not signal → you can't tell what's gaining ground
- Journalist networks give you tips but not systematic coverage → gaps
- Current monitoring is retrospective, not predictive [CRM: "narrative early-warning
  layer" was the hook language that landed]

---

## The unlock (Terminal's role in the job)

Terminal is the early-warning layer that surfaces which narratives and frames
are *moving* — velocity and direction — before they reach mainstream.

**How to position it in copy:**
- Never lead with "AI-powered" or "platform" — these are feature descriptors
- Lead with the job outcome, introduce Terminal as the mechanism in sentence 3+
- The proof point is speed: demo users see signal before it appears in their
  current feeds [CRM: "fullt fungerande" — Rolf van den Brink; implies he tested it
  and it worked as claimed]

## Disqualifiers (copy must NOT target these)

Writing to the wrong audience wastes sends and dilutes ICP signal.
Every email and social post must pass the disqualifier filter:

| Disqualifier | Why | Signal in CRM |
|---|---|---|
| Individual politicians (not party comms) | High gatekeeping, wrong budget authority | 6/6 "Drafted - awaiting approval" — zero conversions |
| One-event institutions (e.g. Gotland municipality) | No recurring monitoring need | 2/2 "Responded - closed" post-Almedalen |
| Generic PR firms (no political/PA clients) | Terminal's value is narrative tracking, not brand comms | Assumption — verify with PA firm follow-up |
| Contacts framing interest as "only for Almedalen" | No standing monitoring mandate | Gotland pattern |

## Language rules (derived from ICP "how they talk")

These are the actual words and frames that landed. Use them literally in copy.

**Use:**
- "narratives gaining or losing traction"
- "before it reaches mainstream coverage"
- "narrative early-warning layer"
- "frames," "framing divergence," "issue ownership"
- "story gaps"
- Swedish subject lines or opening phrases when targeting Swedish media directly
  [CRM: Johan Kling replied in Swedish within 35 min; Rolf van den Brink
  replied same-day multiple times]

**Avoid:**
- "AI-powered" in subject lines (feature framing, not job framing)
- "platform" as a standalone noun
- Generic media monitoring language that any Meltwater competitor could claim
- Passive urgency ("whenever you're ready", "let me know if interested")

## JTBD copy checklist

Every variant produced by the copy-improver skill MUST pass all 7 checks
before it can be presented to the user.

- [ ] **J1 — Job in subject:** Subject line names the trigger, outcome, or
      obstacle. NOT the product name. NOT a generic opener.
- [ ] **J2 — Pain first:** Sentence 1 of the body names their current reality
      (the obstacle or the trigger). The product is not mentioned until
      sentence 3 at the earliest.
- [ ] **J3 — Outcome named:** At least one outcome statement from the list
      above appears in the body — verbatim or in close paraphrase.
- [ ] **J4 — Disqualifier clean:** The email is not addressed to / does not
      imply targeting of individual politicians, one-event institutions, or
      generic PR contacts.
- [ ] **J5 — Language match:** At least one phrase from the "Use" language
      list appears naturally in the body.
- [ ] **J6 — Event hook (if applicable):** If a time-bound event is within
      6 weeks, the copy names it and the deadline. Evergreen framing only
      when no event is imminent.
- [ ] **J7 — CTA is low-friction:** CTA offers concrete evidence of the
      outcome (a demo showing real data, a specific example of a narrative
      shift caught early). NOT "learn more" or "let's chat."
