## Persona A — Literary Agent

### Core job statement

> **When** I'm building my client list and looking for the next wave of epic fantasy,
> **I want to** find a debut author with a fully realized world, a complete first book,
> and a series that publishers will see as IP with franchise potential —
> **so I can** represent a client whose career arc is measurable and whose rights I can sell across multiple formats.

### Functional job
Identify culturally distinctive fantasy series with multimedia IP potential before competitors sign them. Filter out: one-hit debuts without series plans, poorly edited manuscripts, derivative Tolkien clones.

### Emotional job
Feel like a tastemaker who spots the next wave. Agents who signed N.K. Jemisin, Tomi Adeyemi, or Rebecca Yarros early built their reputations on the bet. This agent wants that.

### Social job
Be the agent who brought a breakthrough African-inspired fantasy series to market — at a time when publishers are actively seeking non-Eurocentric voices.

### Outcome statements (what this agent is trying to achieve)
1. "A manuscript complete enough to send to editors without extensive revision"
2. "A series outline showing at least 3 books fully planned"
3. "Clear comp titles that tell me where this book sits in the market and how it sold"
4. "An author who can talk about the world coherently in public"
5. "IP that could realistically be optioned for screen or comics"

### Obstacle (what blocks them today)
Slush pile is 95% derivative. Finding African or ancient civilization-inspired epic fantasy that is genuinely world-built (not a skin swap over European structures) is difficult. Most debut pitches lack multimedia ambition or a series plan beyond book 1.

### Language rules (agent-facing copy)
**Use:** "eight-book series," "original secondary world," "mythology-first world-building," "literary agent ready," "comp titles: Ken Liu's *Dandelion Dynasty*, M.L. Wang's *Sword of Kaigen*," "manuscript complete," "multimedia IP," "visual adaptation potential," "rooted in the Kingdom of Benin, the Mongol Empire, the Moorish empires of Spain, and the Aztec and Inca civilisations"
**Avoid:** "self-published," "posted on Royal Road," "fan fiction influence," "I've already released it," vague superlatives ("unique," "like nothing you've read")

### JTBD checklist (agent query copy)

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| J1 | Does the query subject line name the genre + cultural hook? (Not "Fantasy Novel" — name what makes it distinctive) |
| J2 | Does paragraph 1 name the protagonist's world and the core conflict — NOT the backstory? |
| J3 | Is at least one comp title named that sold in the last 3-5 years? |
| J4 | Does it state word count and series length? |
| J5 | Does it name the agent's specific taste in a personalized line? (Shows research, not mass-blast) |
| J6 | Does it mention multimedia or adaptation potential? (Only if true — don't oversell) |
| J7 | Is there a clear bio line naming any prior publication credits or workshop credentials? |
| J8 | Does it end with a specific, low-friction ask? ("I'd be glad to send the full manuscript") |

## Persona B — ARC Reader / Power Fantasy Reader

### Core job statement

> **When** I finish an epic fantasy series and I'm hungry for something next —
> **I want to** find a new world I've never seen before, written by an author who actually built the mythology from scratch —
> **so I can** invest in a long series (8+ books) with a world I'll return to for years.

### Functional job
Find the next series to commit to before it gets popular. Be a founding reader whose early review matters. Preferably: a world that challenges the Eurocentric defaults of mainstream fantasy.

### Emotional job
Feel like a discoverer. The reader who found Brandon Sanderson before Mistborn hit, who read Nnedi Ofofor before she was mainstream, who reviewed Children of Blood and Bone before the movie deal.

### Social job
Be the person in their reading community who recommends the series that turns out to be the next big thing.

### Outcome statements
1. "I want to read fantasy with world-building that doesn't just rehash medieval Europe"
2. "I want a series long enough that I can stay in the world — not a one-and-done"
3. "I want to feel like an early insider, not a latecomer"
4. "I want to see cultures I recognize, or never recognized, treated with depth"
5. "I want an ARC request that actually explains what makes the book different"

### Obstacle (what blocks them today)
ARC request posts are indistinguishable from each other. "Epic fantasy with a chosen one" tells them nothing. They've read too many books that promised fresh world-building and delivered Tolkien-with-different-names. They are skeptical of debut authors unless there's a compelling hook.

### Language rules (ARC/reader-facing copy)
**Use:** "world-building rooted in [specific culture]," "8-book series," "founding readers," "mythology built from scratch," "here's what makes this world different: [specific]," "no chosen one / no prophecy / [genre differentiator]"
**Avoid:** "unlike anything you've read before" (every ARC post says this), "epic adventure," "magic system" (too generic), vague comparisons

### JTBD checklist (ARC request / reader outreach copy)

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| J1 | Does the post/email name the specific cultural influence upfront? ("rooted in X civilization" not "diverse fantasy") |
| J2 | Does it name one thing the world does that other fantasy doesn't? (The obstacle/differentiator) |
| J3 | Does it state the series length? (8+ books is a feature, not a liability, for committed readers) |
| J4 | Does it say what kind of ARC reader you want? (Honest reviewers, Goodreads active, etc.) |
| J5 | Does it sound like a specific person wrote it — not a template? |
| J6 | Does it make the ask easy and low-friction? (ARC is free, review is optional but appreciated) |
| J7 | Does it give them a reason to say yes now rather than later? (Limited ARC spots, launch date) |

## Persona C — Serialization Reader / Patreon Patron

### Core job statement

> **When** I'm between books and hungry for more content from a world I love —
> **I want to** get early access to chapters and lore that doesn't exist anywhere else —
> **so I can** feel like an insider of the world, not just a reader waiting for the next release.

### Functional job
Access advance chapters (10-15 ahead of public release) and exclusive world content (lore, maps, character backstories). Know what happens next before anyone else.

### Emotional job
Be part of the world-building process. Feel that the author sees them as co-owners of the world's lore, not just consumers.

### Social job
Be the person in their community who knows what's coming next, who the author acknowledges by name, who is in the Discord.

### Outcome statements
1. "Read advance chapters before anyone else"
2. "Get lore content that doesn't appear in any published chapter"
3. "Have the author actually know who I am"
4. "Be part of a community around the world, not just the book"
5. "Know I'm directly funding more books in the series"

### Obstacle (what blocks them today)
Most author Patreons are glorified tip jars with no exclusive content. Or they promise exclusive content and burn out after 3 months. Readers have been let down before.

### Language rules (Patreon/serialization copy)
**Use:** "advance chapters," "lore that doesn't appear in any chapter," "founding patron," "10 chapters ahead of the public," "insider of the world," "classified documents of the world," "you're funding the series"
**Avoid:** "support me," "tip jar," "help me write," vague "exclusive content" without naming what it is, promises you can't sustain (weekly art posts if you're not an illustrator)

### Patreon tier structure (data-grounded)
| Tier | Price | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Lore Insider | $3/month | Monthly lore drop (bestiary entry, map region, historical event) + thank-you in next release |
| Advance Reader | $5/month | 10 advance chapters + all Lore Insider content. **Revenue engine — 70% of patrons should land here.** |
| World Architect | $10-15/month | 15 advance chapters + author commentary on world design + Discord role + name in acknowledgments |

Note: Flat 10% Patreon fee as of August 2025. Price tiers to absorb it. Do NOT promise physical rewards until 200+ patrons.

### JTBD checklist (Patreon launch / serialization posts)

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| J1 | Does the launch post name what's exclusive — specific, not vague? |
| J2 | Does it explain the advance chapter count (10 or 15 ahead)? |
| J3 | Does it name the world/series so the reader knows what they're joining? |
| J4 | Does it give a reason to join now vs later? (Founding patron pricing, early Discord access) |
| J5 | Does it name the posting cadence? (Readers need a schedule they can count on) |
| J6 | Does it acknowledge that patrons are funding more books in the series? |
| J7 | Is the CTA specific? (Not "check out my Patreon" — link + "join at the $5 tier for advance chapters") |

## 10 standing JTBD rules (cross-persona)

1. **Never post Book 1 free online while querying agents.** Free online publishing weakens the commercial proposition for publishers who must pay an advance.
2. **Cultural inspiration is a feature, not a niche.** Name every source specifically: "rooted in the Kingdom of Benin, the Mongol Empire, the Moorish empires of Spain, and the Aztec and Inca civilisations" is searchable and memorable. "African fantasy" is too vague. "Diverse fantasy" is invisible. The specific names are the hook.
3. **8-book series length is a selling point.** To agents: shows IP depth. To readers: justifies the investment in learning the world. To Patreon: shows long-term subscription value.
4. **Comics and animation potential should be mentioned to agents but not overpromised.** Say "designed with adaptation in mind" only if the world has distinct visual identity.
5. **ARC outreach must be specific.** "I wrote a fantasy novel" is noise. "A debut epic fantasy rooted in [specific African civilization] with a [specific differentiator]" cuts through.
6. **Patreon launched same week as any serialization — not after hitting a follower milestone.** 35 active Royal Road Patreons have fewer than 2,000 followers. Early is better.
7. **Avoid IP-risky platforms.** Inkitt, Galatea, Wattpad Studio have rights terms that complicate comics/animation licensing. Use Royal Road, Ream Stories, or self-hosted for any serialization.
8. **Ream Stories preferred over Patreon for fiction.** Better tooling for authors (automated tier releases, 15-minute setup vs 6 hours on Patreon). Consider both; run Ream for serialization and Patreon for broader community.
9. **r/worldbuilding is a reader acquisition channel.** Post lore and cultural systems with no book mention. Build the world's reputation before the book's.
10. **Writers of the Future is the highest-ROI competition for this author.** Free entry, quarterly submission, SFWA-qualifying, the most recognized debut SF/F credential with agents. Enter next cycle immediately.

## Disqualifier table

| Scenario | Action |
|---|---|
| Agent query to agent who reps only literary fiction | Don't send — wasted query slot |
| Copy that mentions Book 1 is available on Royal Road | Stop — conflicts with trad pub query |
| Patreon post promising weekly art, physical merch, or something you can't sustain | Revise down — overpromising kills retention |
| ARC request that doesn't name the cultural inspiration | Rewrite — specificity is the whole hook |
| Copy targeting a platform with rights grabs (Inkitt, Galatea) | Flag immediately — IP protection is cardinal rule |
