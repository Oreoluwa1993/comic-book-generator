## Pre-run standing rules (from research, June 2026)

These rules apply before any campaign data exists. They come from research across Royal Road author communities, r/selfpublish, agent submission guides, and Patreon data (750-story analysis, Chapter Chronicles 2025).

### Agent query copy rules

- **Specificity beats superlatives.** Agents read 50,000+ queries/year. "An epic fantasy unlike anything you've read" means nothing. "An eight-book epic fantasy series rooted in [specific African civilization], where the mythology predates the magic system by 2,000 in-world years" signals a world-builder, not a genre writer.
- **The personalized line matters.** Agents track whether authors did research on their list. One sentence showing you know their recent clients or stated preferences converts cold queries into warm ones.
- **Comp titles are the most important data point.** If comps are wrong (too old, too niche, too bestseller-aspirational), the query gets filed. Use comps from the last 3-5 years that actually sold.
- **Do not mention prior self-publishing or web serial posting** of Book 1 in the query. If asked directly, be honest — but lead with the manuscript.

### ARC reader outreach rules

- **The cultural specificity is the hook.** Every ARC post says "epic fantasy." Almost none say "rooted in [specific civilization] with a magic system derived from [specific mythological tradition]." That's the opening line.
- **Series length converts committed readers.** "Book 1 of 8" to a power reader is a reason to sign up now, not a reason to wait.
- **Ask for honest reviews, not positive ones.** Review credibility matters for Amazon ranking. 50 honest reviews with 3-4 star average converts better than 10 obviously solicited 5-star reviews.
- **NetGalley generates reviews but low community engagement.** r/fantasy, r/fantasywriters, and ARC Facebook groups generate community members who talk about the book. Pursue both.

### Patreon / Ream launch rules

- **Launch same week as first serialized chapter.** Do not wait for a follower milestone. 35 active Patreons with fewer than 2,000 followers. Early is better.
- **Name the advance chapter count specifically.** "10 advance chapters ahead of the public" converts better than "exclusive content."
- **Lore drops are the highest-ROI patron content.** A monthly lore drop — one bestiary entry, one map region, one historical event not in any chapter — takes 1-2 hours to produce and deepens world investment more than any other content type.
- **Ream Stories should be evaluated alongside Patreon.** As of 2026, Ream has better tooling for fiction serialization (automated tier releases, integrated reader community). Consider running both or migrating to Ream if Patreon setup is burdensome.
- **Tier pricing (flat 10% Patreon fee effective August 2025):** $3 (lore insider) / $5 (advance chapters, revenue engine) / $10-15 (world architect with commentary + acknowledgments). No physical rewards until 200+ patrons.

### Community building rules (r/worldbuilding, Discord)

- **Post lore, not the book.** r/worldbuilding posts that describe the mythology, history, or cultural systems of the world — with no mention of a book — build organic followers around the IP itself. This is a long-game reader acquisition channel.
- **Engage in r/fantasy for 2-3 months before mentioning the book.** The community is hostile to cold self-promotion. Become a participant first.
- **Discord works when the author shows up weekly.** A 30-minute "I'm here" session in Discord converts more patrons than complex architecture. Don't over-engineer the server.
