## Competitors analysed

### 1. Meltwater
- **Category:** Global media monitoring (legacy leader)
- **Positioning:** "Media intelligence platform" — volume coverage, global reach, analytics dashboards
- **Channels:** Heavy content marketing, review site presence (G2, Capterra), high ad spend on generic keywords
- **JTBD angle used:** "Stay informed" / "measure your PR impact" — retrospective framing
- **What they don't say:** Nothing about *velocity*, *direction*, or *lead time*. Their alerts fire after coverage lands.
- **Opportunity:** "By the time your Meltwater alert fires, the narrative has already formed."

### 2. Cision
- **Category:** Global PR/media monitoring
- **Positioning:** PR measurement and distribution — focuses on earned media ROI, journalist database
- **JTBD angle used:** "Prove the value of your PR" — metric and reporting framing
- **What they don't say:** Zero narrative direction language. Zero lead-time language.
- **Opportunity:** Cision is a reporting tool, not a signal tool. Different job entirely.

### 3. Talkwalker (now Hootsuite subsidiary)
- **Category:** Social listening + media monitoring
- **Positioning:** "Consumer intelligence" — brand health, social trends, consumer insights
- **JTBD angle used:** Brand and consumer sentiment. Not political. Not narrative direction.
- **What they don't say:** Zero framing/narrative language. Social-first, not news-first.
- **Opportunity:** Talkwalker's acquisition into Hootsuite further dilutes political/PA relevance. Call it out directly.

### 4. Brandwatch
- **Category:** Consumer intelligence
- **Positioning:** Similar to Talkwalker — brand health, social listening, audience insights
- **JTBD angle used:** "Understand your audience" — consumer-facing, not narrative-facing
- **What they don't say:** No political/PA copy visible anywhere.
- **Opportunity:** Same as Talkwalker. Neither is a competitor in political narrative space.

### 5. Signal AI
- **Category:** AI media monitoring — closest AI-native competitor
- **Positioning:** "AI-powered media intelligence" — emerging LLM angle, enterprise focus
- **JTBD angle used:** "Monitor at scale with AI" — they lead with AI capability, not outcome
- **What they don't say:** "Narrative velocity", "lead time", "which frame is winning"
- **Watch:** Signal AI is roadmapping LLM summarisation of media trends. Convergence risk if they add direction/velocity signals.
- **Opportunity:** They lead with the mechanism (AI). Terminal leads with the outcome (narrative early-warning). Mechanistic copy is the losing strategy.

### 6. Retriever (+ Infomedia merger)
- **Category:** Scandinavian media monitoring (legacy)
- **Positioning:** Nordic media archive and monitoring — clipping service heritage
- **JTBD angle used:** "Comprehensive Nordic coverage" — volume and archive access
- **What they don't say:** Zero AI positioning. Zero narrative direction. Zero velocity language.
- **Disruption window:** Retriever+Infomedia merger creates internal turbulence and customer uncertainty. Sales teams are focused on retention, not innovation. **This is a live acquisition window — contact their customers now.**
- **Opportunity:** "Retriever tells you what was published. Terminal tells you what's gaining ground."

### 7. FiscalNote / Quorum / POLITICO Pro
- **Category:** Legislative / political intelligence (US-focused)
- **Positioning:** Policy tracking, regulatory monitoring, political risk — US legislative database
- **JTBD angle used:** "Track legislation and policy risk" — compliance and government affairs framing
- **What they don't say:** None have European/Swedish political narrative tracking. None have media narrative framing.
- **Opportunity:** Different job. FiscalNote et al track *what lawmakers do*. Terminal tracks *what narratives are moving*. Zero overlap in ICP.

### 8. Factal / Dataminr
- **Category:** Real-time event intelligence (breaking news)
- **Positioning:** First-alert on breaking events — speed of detection for safety/finance use cases
- **JTBD angle used:** "Be first to know when something happens" — event detection, not narrative analysis
- **What they don't say:** No narrative framing, no direction/velocity signals, no political PA use case
- **Opportunity:** Factal catches the event. Terminal catches the narrative before the event is the story. Complementary tools, different jobs.

## Market narrative (what everyone is saying)

> **"Monitor everything so you can act faster."**

Every competitor from Meltwater to Signal AI is selling some variant of this claim. It is the saturated default. Copy that echoes this framing disappears into the noise.

The critical gap: **direction and velocity**. Not "what was published" (Retriever) or "how much coverage" (Meltwater) or "when something breaks" (Factal). **Which narratives are gaining ground — before they surface in mainstream.**

## Standing rules (updated 2026-06-27)

These rules apply to all copy produced by copy-improver for Cintelytics/Terminal. Hard constraints.

1. **Never lead with "AI-powered"** — Signal AI owns this lane and it's mechanism framing, not outcome framing. Every competitor mentions AI. Terminal leads with the outcome.
2. **Never use "comprehensive coverage" or "all sources"** — Meltwater, Retriever, Cision all claim this. It's table stakes positioning, not differentiation.
3. **Never echo "stay informed" or "monitor everything"** — this is the saturated market claim. It implies reactive, not predictive.
4. **Name the disruption window** — Retriever+Infomedia merger is live. Contact their Scandinavian customers now with: "Retriever tells you what was published. Terminal tells you what's gaining ground."
5. **Lead with velocity and direction, not volume** — "which narratives are gaining" beats "how much coverage". Volume is a feature. Direction is the job.
6. **Swedish-language subject or opener for Swedish media/PA targets** — two confirmed fast responders. Treat as default for Stockholm/Göteborg media contacts.
